KSA Petrochemical Sector
Spreads and Sentiments Improving
▪
Key feedstock Propane and Butane are likely to see the third consecutive quarterly
decline in Q1 ‘23, alleviating cost pressures.
▪
On the other hand, despite recessionary fears, product prices have shown resilience on
China opening – 4Q ’22 product prices likely being the bottom and Q1 ‘23 higher so far.
▪
With Global chemical stocks rallying in January, our focus is on companies which have
value beyond just an improvement in Petchem spreads with preferred names being
Kayan and Alujain. Current P/B for KSA Petchems is ~20% below 3-year averages.
Global chemicals rallying: A recovery in China’s economy could provide impetus to Petchem
demand after Chinese PMI remained mostly below 50 in 2022 because of zero-COVID policy
forcing the country to go into shutdowns. Global Petchems (BASF/Dow/Lyondell Basel etc.)
have rallied around 15% in Jan. Other possible upside drivers to watch include possible revival
in European (as gas prices have declined to mid-June 2021 levels) and Asian economies (led by
India/Japan), Russian-Ukrainian conflict resolution, Fed rates softening etc. Overall, we see
sentiments improving in the sector.
Coverage: The companies chosen in this report have value more than just an upside in spreads.
They have drivers such as expansions or are beaten-down stocks that allow entry at reasonably
decent levels for a longer-term positioning even if complete recovery takes longer. For example,
despite the volatility in profits, Kayan generates strong cashflows yielding FCF of ~15% (’22).
Alujain has a single product portfolio, but the stock is likely misunderstood and mispriced.
Despite concrete expansion plans of Advanced Petrochemicals, the stock is valued today based
only on its current capacity. Likewise, Sipchem is also planning expansions and has attractive
dividend yields even after factoring in the decline in spreads. Overall, while there are risks, we
believe the downside in these names is lower. We favour Kayan and Alujain among the four.

Sector wide risks to consider
▪
A common risk across the Saudi petchem firms is the possibility of an increase in feedstock
pricing in KSA. While it depends on the extent of hike, it could take another 3 years in our
view. With a decline in gas prices globally the difference between global and Saudi gas prices
has narrowed weakening the case for an increase.
▪
Chinese expansion adding Olefins capacity is another threat to the margins. However, as
explained by management of various companies, there have been continuous delays in the
past which may continue.
▪
Spike in logistics costs as witness in the past may reduce the profit margins of the producers.

Alujain Corp.
(ALCO AB Equity)

Key investment themes
▪
Beaten down: Despite being a straightforward single-product company (producing
Polypropylene from Propane), the company is less widely tracked and likely suffers from
information bias in our view. This may also have contributed to the higher-than-average
decline in stock prices recently. For example, Alujain declined ~2 % vs A C’s ~37% in the
last 1 year despite both having similar portfolio mix.
▪
Expansion plans: The company has received feedstock allocation approval from Ministry of
Energy to build plants in Yanbu industrial city. The plan involves a propane gas processing
plant to produce Polypropylene (600k tonnes) with a total cost of SAR7.5bn from 1H 2026.
▪ Cheap valuations: In a cyclical sector, P/B may be a better relative valuation multiple
indicator than P/E especially considering the volatility in earnings. The company currently
trades at a P/B of 0.8x vs 3-year average of 1.6x (Bloomberg).
▪ Prior Shareholder issues mostly resolved: After certain shareholder-related issues in the
past, the company is progressing well as per expectations. The main cash cow NATPET is
consolidated, and the company has also delivered an increase in dividends.
▪ NATPET stake increase. Most recently the company raised stake from 57.4% in 2021 to
currently 76.4% - the most recent acquisition at a price of SAR48/share. This implies the
management expects there is significant value in the stock with today’s price at around
SAR41/share. The price had also reached SAR80/share at its peak in 2022. We expect the
stake in NATPET to increase further which would increase dividends too.
Risk factors to consider
▪
Holding company costs: Though the company plans to increase its stake in NATPET,
currently the holding company management costs are additional costs as compared to a
direct ownership in NATPET.
▪
Dividends: Despite net income of SAR200mn in the last 12 months, the company paid only
SAR104mn as dividends (~50% payout) to the shareholders. If the payout ratio does not
increase, market may not push the valuation higher despite value in the stock.
▪
Clarity on treasury shares: There requires to be additional clarity on the treatment of the
treasury shares. Lack of sufficient information may weigh on the stock.
▪
Shutdowns: Given that its closest competitor in terms of product mix is APPC, usually
shutdowns are compared with that of APPC. Thus, there might be valuation discount
applied on Alujain if number of shutdowns are frequent (APPC sets the benchmark with
one shutdown every three years).
▪
Expansions: Expansions come with its own risks. Such a huge expansion planned could dent
profitability in its early years especially when interest rates are high.

Valuation
We value the company based on an equal blend of EV/EBITDA and DCF valuation. We use an
EV/EBITDA of 10x (3Y avg is 10.5x) to arrive at a multiple based 12 month forward valuation of
SAR57.4/share. For DCF we use a WACC of 9.5% and cost of equity of 10.5% and arrive at a 12-
month forward DCF valuation of SAR49.3/share. Our target price of SAR53/share (rounded) has
an implied dividend yield of 4.9% (2023) and 6.1% (2024). Given the 29% upside, we initiate
coverage with an Overweight rating.

Company profile
Alujain was established in 1991. The company's activities encompass managing its subsidiaries
or participating in the management and providing support to companies in which it owns
shares. The company carries out its activities as a holding company.
The company and its subsidiaries are involved in the production and sale of polypropylene (the
input being propane), and its derivatives, establishment, operation, and investment in industrial
projects, including projects related to the petrochemical and chemical industries, basic and
transformational industries, plastic industries (plastics), industries related to renewable energy
and other vital industries inside and outside the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.
Most of the group's net revenue is derived from manufacturing Polypropylene.

NATPET: Alujain owns majority equity (76.4%) in NATPET which operates approximately
400,000 tons per year of Propylene & Polypropylene Complex (PP Complex) in Yanbu Industrial

NATPET also owns four companies: Menna Company, Fawasel Advanced Chemicals Company,
Al-Taj Towers for Plastic Industries Company, and Afaq Professional Chemicals Company.
On 11 November 2021, the Group obtained control over NATPET thus becoming a subsidiary.
NATPET is the cash cow for the firm and Alujain has been steadily increasing its ownership in
NATPET and now owns 76.4% of the company. The company plans to further increase its stake
in NATPET. This is positive to avoid minority leakage in the company.

main business includes the production and marketing of homecare products (spray starch, air-
fresheners, fabric softener, oven cleaner, furniture polish, anti-static spray, bleach, stainless
steel polish, etc.), insecticides and agricultural pesticides, fungicides, herbicides, etc. of all kinds
and forms, besides general health products. It does not contribute materially to the group (see

Expansion Plans
The company is planning to establish a new petrochemical project in Yanbu Industrial City. The
new project comprises a propane gas processing plant to manufacture propylene, a plant to
produce polypropylene, and a complex to produce polypropylene compounds and PP specialty
materials. It will also include a grid connecting the propylene and polypropylene production
units of NATPET to the two production units in the Yanbu project for scale and efficiency.
The project is expected to produce more than 600,000 tons/year of polypropylene,
polypropylene compounds, specialized construction materials from polypropylene derivatives,
and about 25,000 tons/year of salable hydrogen. Thus it will more than double existing capacity.
The new project is expected to cost SAR7.5bn and come on stream by 1H26.
More details:
Alujain, like Advanced Petrochemicals, manufactures Polypropylene from Propane with a
similar capacity but uses a different technology to manufacture Polypropylene. Its plant
operating rates are healthy but lower than Advanced Petrochemicals. We believe it is still
early days for the company to assess its operating rates given that it is a new plant.
Polypropylene is a downstream petrochemical product derived from the olefin monomer
propylene. The polymer is produced through a process of monomer connection called addition
polymerization. The company's products are the basic building blocks used to produce daily
used goods, like personal care products, fresh food packaging, durable textiles, medical
applications, automotive components, lightweight plastics, construction materials, etc.
The key product prices/spread to track are Polypropylene and Propane.
Polypropylene's supply and demand situation has been weak in the global market. The pricing
has been impacted due to the slowdown in global economic growth. Global tension due to the
ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict, China's stringent COVID policy, and supply chain disruptions
have put prices and demand under pressure. The long-term outlook for the industry remains
favorable though.

Saudi Kayan Petrochemical Co.
(Kayan AB Equity)

Key investment themes
▪
Solid cash flows – leading to continuous significant debt reduction: The company has
reported an average annual operating cash flow of SAR3bn in the last 5 years and has been
a great deleveraging story. With it, the company repaid debts of SAR 13 billion during the
same period (average 2.6bn annually). This compares to only a current market cap of
SAR20bn. Thereby once debt reaches manageable levels, this could all be paid out as
dividends. Without factoring for time value, this translates to a comfortable 13% yield
which gives a margin of safety for a longer-term value proposition.
▪
Focus on cashflows and not net profit: The company has higher-than-usual depreciation
for a company of its size. In the last 4 years the average depreciation is SAR2.3bn as
compared to maintenance capex of SAR300mn. Thus, net profit is very volatile and makes
the P/E look high. One needs to look at the cash flows than income statement profits for
the company.
▪
Increased allocation of Ethane: Saudi Kayan expects an increased allocation of ethane that
will reduce dependence on Butane which the company has remained sensitive to. This
would enhance the company’s ability to address market volatility and report better
profitability.
▪
Diversified Product mix: Unlike Kayan and Alujain, the company has a diversified product
mix.
Risk factors to consider
▪
Uneven nature of profits and sensitivity to Butane: Given the high contribution of Butane
and high fixed costs, the company is very sensitive to Butane prices. The high operating
leverage has resulted in the company seeing high volatility in its bottom line in the past.
While investors need to focus on cashflows instead of net profits, this tends to weaken
sentiments on the stock.
▪
Disclosures: The company reported record quarterly losses in Q3 2022 but has not given
detailed disclosures on the reasons for the same. Such information gaps weigh on
sentiments.
▪
No dividends: We do not expect dividends as the company is focusing on growth and likely
expansions. Also, it may take 2-3 years for dividends to be paid if there are no expansions.
▪
Ethane allocation: Ethane allocation could take a long time. While there is concrete news
on the allocation of additional ethane, this could also be a substitution project whereby a
part of butane (12%) will be replaced by ethane so that the plants are already capable to
use ethane and butane interchangeably. Thus, this may not have additional output capacity
but will improve margins as the company moves from high-cost Butane to low-cost Ethane.
It will take at least 2 years for the company to receive this allocation and might incur some
capex as well.

Company Profile
Advanced Petrochemical Company ("Advanced") was founded in 2005 and listed on Tadawul in
2007. The company was initially launched by National Polypropylene Limited, jointly owned by
Khalifa Al Mulhim, now the Chairman of the company.
The company manufactures petrochemicals, including propylene (455,000 tons/year) which is
used in turn to manufacture polypropylene (450,000 tons/year of name plate capacity) though
the actual production is much higher. The company has its production facility in Jubail Industrial
City. It started the construction of its plants in May 2005 and commenced commercial
production in March 2008.
The company has a presence across MENA, Europe, India, Asia Pacific, Turkey, and China. It
markets and sells its products through international off-takers like Vinmar International,
Mitsubishi Corporation, and Domo N.V. Most revenues come from overseas sales representing
~93% of total revenues.
The company is known for actively shifting sales of its products towards markets that have
better pricing or are in demand. For example, the company increased its sales exposure towards
Turkey up from 15% in 2015 to ~30% in 2019 as pricing improved. Accordingly, the company
was involved in making material (woven PP) required for masks during COVID. he company’s
solid management is widely acknowledged as the best in the sector in KSA.

prices, the cyclicality could work in favour of Sipchem once Chinese economy rebounds.

Company profile
Sahara International Petrochemical Company (Sipchem), established in 1999, is one of the
leading petrochemical companies in the world. The company is listed on Tadawul and included
in the MSCI Emerging Markets Index. It is the second largest petrochemical player in Saudi after
SABIC by market capitalization.
The company owns and manages a petrochemicals complex at Jubail Industrial City. The
complex is strategically located in the eastern region due to the availability of raw materials and
required petrochemical products in the region. The complex has proximity to the King Fahd
Industrial Port and the Jubail Commercial Port, which proves to be beneficial for exports.
The company has a diversified product portfolio. It produces a wide range of products, from
basic chemicals and polymers to specialty chemicals. It manufactures and markets methanol
(MeOH), butanediol (BDO), tetra hydro furan, acetic acid (AA), maleic anhydride, and vinyl
acetate monomer (VAM), as well as carbon monoxide through its various subsidiaries, among
others. This is different from most of the other companies in KSA that make basic polymers as
seen from the Figure below.

The company reports segmental revenue in the following format:
1) Basic chemicals (which includes Methanol and BDO) which contributed 31% of
revenues but 42% of operating profits profits in 2021.
2) Intermediate (which includes mainly VAM, Caustic Soda, EDC) contributed 44% of
revenues and 36% of operating profit
3) Polymers (mainly EVA) contributed 33% of revenues and 18% of operating profits.
4) The other division called marketing mainly buys internally and sells to customers
externally.

Modelling the company is complex because of the interdependence of products among the
companies. For example, Methanol is sold externally as well as used to produce AA which is
inturn used to produce VAM. The ownership of each plant is different adding to the
complexity. Products such as BDO do not have easily available prices such as other products.
The company has been increasing its stake in the subsidiaries that would avoid minority leakage.
Sipchem has increased its stake in IDC (to 100% from 54%), enabling it to take strategic
initiatives in the subsidiaries. The company acquired the remaining 3% stakes in International
Acetyl Company Limited and International Vinyl Acetate Company, increasing its ownership in
both firms to 100%. Current ownerships are shown in the Figure above.

As discussed above, Methanol (MeOH) is primarily converted to formaldehyde, which has many
applications, especially in polymers. Acetic acid (AA) can be produced from methanol. MeOH
combined with isobutene makes MTBE a major octane booster in gasoline (petrol). It can also
be used as a clean energy source.
Ethyl Acetate (EA) is used in nail varnish removers to clean circuit boards, decaffeinated coffee
beans and tea leaves, paints and perfumes, among other applications. Butanediol (BDO)
industrial use is a solvent in manufacturing certain plastics, elastic fibres, and polyurethanes.
AA is used to manufacture paints, adhesives, inks, coatings etc. Ethylene-vinyl acetate (EVA) is
used to manufacture car mats, artificial flowers, surfboards, plastic films etc. Sodium hydroxide
(NaOH), called caustic soda, can make soaps, rayons, paper, explosives, dyes, and petroleum
products. Vinyl Acetate Monomer (VAM) is a significant intermediate used in producing a wide
range of resins and polymers for paints & coatings, adhesives, glues & sealants, elastomers,
textile finishes, paper coatings, binders, films, and a myriad of other industrial and consumer
applications. Polypropylene (PP) is used in plastic packaging, plastic parts for machinery and
equipment, fibres and textiles, electrical insulation, food containers, carpets, ropes, etc.

The pricing for all products has witnessed a gradual decline in 2022-YTD due to the slowdown
in global economic growth. China's zero COVID policy, Russia-Ukraine conflict, and supply chain
disruptions have contributed to the weakness in demand and consequently led to downward
pressure on the prices. The rising inflation across the globe attracted aggressive monetary policy
tightening from major central banks, which has fueled concerns about a recession in the near
to medium term. However, the potential easing of lockdowns in China and the positive signs
from the automotive industry could support the price recovery. The long-term outlook for the
industry remains favorable.
