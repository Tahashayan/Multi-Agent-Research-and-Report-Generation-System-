Arabian Drilling Co.
To benefit from Oil & Gas Capex upcycle
▪
Arabian Drilling was the largest land drilling and offshore contractor in 2021 (with 45
available rigs / ~17% market share) in Saudi Arabia, with six decades of experience.
▪
Recovery from 2020 lows followed by additional contract wins likely to see a ~40%
increase in the number of active rigs by 2025, along with a significant improvement in
day rates implying ~16% CAGR in revenues and ~28% CAGR in profits during 2021-25.
▪
We initiate coverage on Arabian Drilling with a target price of SAR144/share (weighted
average of DCF and P/E - 18x - valuation) and OW rating. We believe the company
stands a good chance to be included in MSCI/FTSE EM indices.
Recovery underway from 2020 lows: In 2019, the company, one of the leading oil drilling
services providers in KSA, had 44 active rigs and 100% utilization of 38 onshore rigs. However,
as a result of the pandemic and temporary rig suspensions by Aramco, operational rigs fell to
35 in 2020. Renewal of suspended contracts and 4 recently awarded contracts implies
contracted rigs could increase up to 44 by the end of 2022. There is visibility for medium/long
term growth as key customer Aramco is expected to increase its oil (13mmbpd MSC by 2027)
and gas production (+50% by 2030) with its Capex to increase from ~US$32bn (2021) to
~US$48bn by 2023e (Bbg. Consensus). Not just the number of rigs, day rates have been
improving significantly and average pricing would pick up as rigs come up for renewal. Thus, we
estimate the operating margin to likely jump from 17% in 2021 to ~21% in 2022e.
Healthy track record: Track record of 60+ years, highly efficient operations, long contractual
periods, consistently high Rig Efficiency Index score (above 90%), and diversification of vendors
for Aramco implies assured growth in business even in a highly competitive market. The
company’s relatively low leverage of 1.1x net debt/EBITDA (2021) also testifies to financial
discipline. The dividend payout could be 80% of its earnings from 2023 onwards.
Risks: Global economic slowdown/recession caused by geopolitical issues/high-interest rate
environment, short-term stagnation in oil demand due to production cut measures adopted by
OPEC+, oversupply in rig market leading to fall in day rates, high dependency on Saudi Aramco,
environmental concerns and change in the energy mix are key risks to our investment view.

Investment case
The company was the largest land drilling and offshore contractor (with 45 available rigs or 17%
market share in 2021) in Saudi Arabia and Saudi Arabia-Kuwait Neutral Zone. The company was
the second largest in terms of the number of land rigs, with a 19% market share (38 rigs) in
Saudi Arabia (2021). The company was the third largest in terms of offshore rigs in Saudi Arabia
(12% of the market with 7 rigs in 2021). In the broader MENA region, the company is the 8th
largest in terms of the number of rigs. The company operates mainly in KSA and seeks to expand
in the GCC region.

In terms of operating history, the company's operations span six decades, with Aramco being a
key customer since 1972. In general, the company has several marquee customers such as Saudi
Aramco (the World's largest oil producer, ~49% of total rigs), Al Khafji Joint Operations (a joint
venture between Aramco and Kuwait Gulf Oil company, 7% of rigs), Baker Hughes (4% of rigs)
and Schlumberger (the world's largest oil field services company, which is now rebranded as
SLB, 33% of rigs). With continuous renewal, most of the rigs have been in operation for a long
term (even as high as 20+ years) with general rigs’ firm contractual period of 3-5 years.

Driven by Aramco’s stability, the oil drilling market in KSA also has historically been resilient to
oil price fluctuations and market cycles. The oil drilling market in the Middle East is also a high
entry barrier market, with a pre-qualification process spanning multiple years. In such a market,
the company’s existing track record is a strategic advantage for future growth. The company
(pre-IPO) was owned 51% by Taqa (in turn owned by 45.75% PIF/ 4.6% Aramco/ 5.6% GOSI etc.)
and 49% by Schlumberger. The company is also looking to expand operations beyond Saudi
Arabia and has submitted tender proposals for projects in Bahrain and Kuwait. Saudi Aramco
itself has growth plans - to increase its maximum sustainable capacity (MSC) to 13mmbpd (from
12mmbpd) by 2027 and gas production by 50% by 2030, with a 50% increase in Capex levels
from 2021 to 2023e.

Most of the company’s 45 rigs are either operational or about to start operations (i.e., already
contracted but not active). In terms of rigs, 37 rigs out of 45 rigs were contracted and utilized in
2021. Out of the remaining 8 rigs, one offshore rig resumed operations in April 2022, and three
onshore rigs resumed drilling by June 2022. The company has also received intimation for
resuming another onshore rig (expected by September 2022, as per prospectus) while contract
negotiations were underway for another rig. The company has also received an additional
contract for four offshore rigs. The remaining two rigs are idle and require upgrades before they
can be deployed for use, while the company is looking to sell one rig. Given the significant
under-investment in exploration Capex globally, day rates had been declining in the past.
However, with an increase in demand and O&G Capex, drilling rates are rising in a significant
manner (as high as 35-40% for offshore rigs and 5-7% for onshore rigs), which is expected to
help Arabian Drilling as and when rig contracts come up for renewal.

The company has strong technical capabilities and a significant depth of experience in the region
spanning several decades. One instance of this capability was evidenced in 2018 when it put
into operation 14 new rigs acquired in 2018 and 2 rigs acquired before 2018 within only one
year of acquisition. The large scale of operations is also evident from the fact that the company
drilled 340 wells in the single year of 2021, while on average, 275 wells were drilled per annum
for the last three years.

Assumptions, valuation, and risks
Revenue:
The company derives revenues by deploying rigs for medium to short-term contracts to oil and
gas companies and project integrators such as SLB with 3/5-year firm periods and 1/2-year
extension options. These rigs are contracted on a 'day rate', which is determined by factors such
as market conditions (rig oversupply or shortage), the competition's intensity, and competitors'
willingness to submit lower bids, among others. Offshore rigs are leased at higher rates than
onshore rigs but are also more expensive in terms of Capex as well as Opex. We expect the
company to increase its rig count to 47 and 49 by the end of 2023 and 2024, respectively, from
current levels of 45 in 2022e. Average day rates are expected to increase from 2021 levels as
the temporary discount given in some rigs’ day rates to Aramco is expected to be reversed. Even
globally, day rates have been improving significantly in some cases (35-40% for offshore rigs
and 5-7% for onshore rigs). We believe the company has a medium-term target of 65 to 70 rigs
to be achieved by 2026 (vs our estimate of 53 rigs by the same period), driven by a foray into
other GCC markets (which we have not factored).

In 2021-25, we expect drilling revenues to witness a CAGR of 17.9%. Apart from the drilling
revenue, the revenues from other businesses like rig movements, mobilization, and others are
expected to see modest growth. Hence, Arabian Drilling’s revenue in 2021-25 is forecasted at a
CAGR of ~16%. For example, the rig move revenue stream is typically dependent on the level of
drilling activity in the Kingdom and the customer’s instructions as to specific locations within
the Kingdom where drilling is planned and may be realized at various stages of a drilling
contract.

Valuation
The nature of the company's business is volatile, with external market conditions driving
demand and competition dictating pricing. We expect a higher equity risk premium and a higher
beta than the market to account for the business risk. We assume a cost of equity of 11% for
our DCF valuation.

For P/E valuation, we use an 18x multiple on 2024 EPS of 7.9/share and arrive at a target of
142/share. As for DCF, we get a SAR146/share as a target based on a terminal growth of 2% and
a WACC of 8.5% with a target capital structure of 35% debt to asset. We use equal weights of
50% each for DCF and P/E valuation, which gives us a weighted average target price of
SAR144/share and thus we initiate on the company with an Overweight rating. Given that the
target price is likely to exceed the minimum requirements for MSCI/FTSE inclusions, there could
be a momentum-based rally in the stock before the inclusion dates.

Risks
Global economic slowdown or recession: The ongoing Russia-Ukraine conflict has severely
impacted the global supply chains and increased inflationary pressures. The central banks
worldwide have responded to it with a persistent rise in benchmark rates leading to higher
concerns of a recession. Such a scenario could lead to a decline in crude oil demand and
destabilize oil prices.

Short-term demand stagnation due to production cut by OPEC+: The production cut strategy
adopted by OPEC+ to stabilize the oil prices could lead to short-term demand stagnation, which
would be detrimental to the demand for services of Arabian Drilling.

Oversupply in the rig market: A potential oversupply scenario in the KSA rig market could lead
to a drop in the day rates and consequently lead to a decline in revenues. However, high barriers
to entry due to Saudi Aramco's stringent drilling contractor regulations should help avoid such
a scenario.

High dependency on Saudi Aramco: Most of the company's revenues and backlog are
dependent on Saudi Aramco. Though the company commands a strong reputation with this key
customer, the high dependency on a single customer makes its topline and future earnings
potential vulnerable to the quality of the relationship between the two firms.

Environmental concerns: The oil industry is typically associated with the phenomenon of global
warming and climate change. The company has policies and procedures to mitigate any
ecological risks related to its operations. However, the nature of business has an inherent
vulnerability to environmental mishaps that could lead to significant financial and reputational
loss to the company.

Change in energy mix: Economies across the globe are gradually adopting alternative energy
sources and reducing their dependency on oil and gas. Though the shift is expected to remain
slow but steady, any faster-than-expected pace of change in the current energy mix would be
detrimental to the demand for the company's services.

Company Profile
Arabian Drilling is a leader in a highly competitive industry with a total fleet of available 45 rigs
in 2022e and a 17% market share, including 35 onshore active rigs and 7 offshore (jack-up)
active rigs. Over the past six decades, Arabian Drilling has become the region's leading provider
of drilling services. From only 1 offshore drilling rig in 1964, it reached a total of 9 rigs (6 land
rigs, 3 offshore rigs) in 2000, and at the end of 2018, with the largest expansion in Arabian
Drilling's history, the company had a total of 43 rigs. Aramco became a client of the company in
1972. PIF, through TAQA, became one of the major shareholders in 2003.

The revenue for the company comes from three major companies, which are Aramco,
Schlumberger, and KJO, with a share of revenue of 54.2%, 39.1%, and 6.3%, respectively, in
2021. However even Schlumberger and KJO also indirectly drive their business through Aramco.
The key business segments of the company are:

Onshore drilling: This segment includes the provision of drilling and related services through
onshore rigs. Onshore drilling is a mechanical process in which a well is drilled onshore through
the underlying bedrock utilizing a stationary land rig to explore and extract oil or natural gas.
The Group's onshore fleet ranges from medium-heavy to ultra-heavy rigs capable of drilling
complicated horizontal wells and operating in harsh environments such as in the Middle East.
The Group also provides rig relocation, mobilization, catering, labor, and other services within
this segment. More than 50% of its rigs are less than 10 years old, and more than 75% of its
onshore rigs feature high-quality specifications with 2,000 horsepower or more. The cost for a
rig is between SAR112.5-225mn depending on the capacity and complexity, with an average day
rate as of 2021 of SAR124,606 (due to temporary discounts for a few rigs in 2021). The average
day rate as of 2020 was SAR129,344.

Offshore drilling: This segment includes the provision of drilling and related services by offshore
rigs. The Group's offshore drilling services include ultra-heavy jack-up rigs equipped with highly
specialized equipment capable of drilling in water depths of up to 375 feet (115 meters). In the

Company Strategy
Increase market share locally and regionally: The company’s largest customer, Saudi Aramco,
plans to raise its MSC production to 13mn barrels of oil equivalent per day by 2027, which would
drive the demand for Arabian Drilling’s services. Moreover, Saudi Aramco is implementing
multiple upstream projects, such as Farzan, Berri, Ain Dar, Hawiyah Unayzah, and Marjan
reservoirs, which would expand the production capacity. Saudi Aramco is also developing the
Kingdom’s largest unconventional non-associated gas field, Jafurah. This field is the region's
biggest liquids-rich shale gas development, with a capacity of about 200,000bn standard cubic
feet of gas. The project would cater to the growing demand for petrochemical feedstock. Apart
from leveraging on the domestic energy industry’s growth, the company would also explore
opportunities in Kuwait and Bahrain and M&As.

Strengthen the workforce of international standards with high Saudization: The company
commands a Saudization rate of 76.2% and is committed to maintaining a high level by
recruiting, training, and retaining local talent in the form of a highly skilled workforce.

Maintain industry-leading quality of services: Arabian Drilling is focused on providing high-

The company’s rigs have been ranked as superior, and high-performance, and the company has

Cost optimization: The company has continued efforts to streamline operations that support a
lean and adjustable cost structure. This allows Arabian Drilling to mitigate any negative impact
from major fluctuations in the business cycle.

Rig Efficiency Index (REI) and In-Kingdom Total Value Add (IKTVA)
REI is an important operational metric used by Saudi Aramco that measures the performance
of each rig provided by its contracted and sub-contracted drilling contractors. REI is determined
based on these four parameters:
1. Health, safety, and environment (HSE)
2. Non-productive time
3. Drilling flat time
4. IKTVA (In-Kingdom Total Value Add)

IKTVA is a program by Saudi Aramco that aims to drive gross added value creation in KSA. The
program seeks to drive innovation and diversification of the industry in the Kingdom, improve
its global competitiveness, and provide quality jobs to the local population.

The REI scores (individual scores are an average of the past 36 months) are published monthly
by Saudi Aramco in its REI performance report, which also ranks and categorizes the rigs into
performance levels of “Superior Performance”, “High Performance”, “Consistent Performance”,
“Average Performance”, and “Below Average Performance”. The likelihood of a drilling
contractor’s contract renewal is based on its REI. The REI score also determines the contractor's
eligibility to participate in Saudi Aramco tenders and the scope to negotiate a renewal or
extension. Arabian Drilling has been a consistent REI outperformer and has been recognized by
Saudi Aramco as a leading rig fleet.

Market dynamics
Drilling contractors in the MENA region can be divided into four distinct segments. First, a
segment of contractors is identified as national/joint venture contractors, the segment to which
the Group belongs. These companies are either owned or partially owned by national or state-
owned companies. However, some contractors have very well-established relationships with
national oil companies and, in some cases, benefit from preferential contractual frameworks.
Other segments include regional contractors, international contractors, and premium
international drilling contractors, which are distinguished by their ability to achieve higher
pricing due to perceived higher quality of service and maintenance of equipment.

There are a variety of players in the KSA drilling market, with no contractor having a market
share of more than 30%. Arabian Drilling competes with other players on the market like SANAD
(which is a JV between ARAMCO and NABORS), Saipem, and other companies to win contracts.
As of 2021, the total Onshore and offshore rigs in KSA and the natural zone was 261 (comprising
59 offshore and 202 onshore rigs). Arabian Drilling is the second largest company in terms of
available onshore rig fleet size, with 38 rigs accounting for 19% of the market share in Saudi
Arabia, behind SANAD, which has 22% with 44 rigs. In terms of offshore drilling rigs, the
company ranks third with 7 rigs, representing 12% of the market share, while ARO drilling is the
largest with 16 rigs, representing 27% of the market share. in terms of the total fleet (onshore
and offshore), Arabian Drilling is the largest contractor in Saudi Arabia, with 45 rigs. In the MENA
region, ADNOC Drilling, a subsidiary of Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC), is the largest
company, with 112 total rigs in operation.

Statistics show that the global exploration and production sector spending in 2021 was
estimated at USD43bn, with USD33bn going to onshore and offshore drilling companies in
shallow waters and the other USD10bn going to drilling in deep waters.

As of 2021, the global spending on onshore shallow water drilling contractors is USD23bn, and
the spending in KSA is estimated at USD1.4bn (6%). Furthermore, intensified drilling for
unconventional gas at the Jafurah field is expected to drive demand for further incremental
onshore drilling rigs from 2021 to 2030. The cost to run a drilling rig per day in (GCC) can reach
up to USD56,000 per day, while the cost for the rig is between SAR112.5-225mn depending on
the capacity and complexity.

Exploration activity in the Middle East is predominantly land-based. However, because onshore
resources are maturing, GCC countries are actively spending on offshore exploration and
starting with the easier, shallow-water drilling. For offshore drilling, the global spending was
estimated at USD10bn, and KSA's share was 14% or USD1.3bn. An offshore oil rig cost is much
higher than the onshore, starting at SAR375mn and going as high as SAR1bn. The cost to run a
drilling rig per day is between USD600,000 to USD800,000

Total E&P spending in the MENA region on onshore and offshore shallow drilling is expected to
grow at a compound annual growth rate of 5% between 2021 to 2025, as per Rystad Energy.
On the other hand, KSA intends to increase its production capacity to 13.0mn barrels of oil
equivalent per day by 2027. These ambitions will require an increase in total E&P spending on
drilling contractors. Thus, KSA is expected to increase spending on onshore and offshore
shallow-water drilling contractors at an annual growth rate of 14% between 2021 to 2025.
