THE HOME OF GOOD FOOD IS MOVING BEYOND LEVELS
Investment Summary:
Solid operating performance amid unprecedented disruption:
Despite the economic recession in Q2 and Q3 of 2020 of -6.10% and -3.62%,
respectively, Flour Mill of Nigeria Plc (FMN Plc) grew its revenue significantly by
34.48% y/y to N771.6 billion in the fiscal year-end, March 2021 (FY-2021). Amidst this
blistering topline growth which was majorly driven by improved volume that
complemented continued local demand for food products, agro-allied, and sugar,
FMN Plc reported an impressive net margin of 3.33% with a 126.05% y/y growth in
net profit (N25 billion) in FY-2021 (N11 billion in FY-2020). This shows the resilience
of the firm to withstand economic shock and maximize value creation for its
stakeholders. Dividend pay-out was maintained at 50.79% on average with a dividend
coverage capacity of 3.13x from FY-2017 to FY-2021. We believe the firm’s agro-allied
strategy and local content investment, and a consumer-driven cheap-staple-food
market which we consider to be largely under-tapped when compared to peers will
continue to spur growth and enhance shareholder’s return.
Diversified Investment Portfolio over 60 years of operation:
Flour Mill of Nigeria Plc is one of the leading FMCG and a top market player in the
flour-based product market. It is the first company to have constructed Nigeria’s first
wheat mill plant. With 60 years of success celebration, the firm has been able to
diversify its core business of food through backward integration into other three
revenue-generating segments which include Agro-allied, Sugar, and support services.
Growth in these three classes of revenue has been impressive at a CAGR of 23.58%
over the past five years. These three segments reported total revenue of N293 billion
(or 38.01% of total revenue) in FY-2021 compared to the N102 billion (or 19.4% of
total revenue) in FY-2017. The firm’s ability to expand its portfolio size with an
explosive growth of this 23.58% CAGR validates its capacity to provide long-term
value creation to shareholders while pursuing a de-risk business opportunity.
Trusted Iconic Brand with Brilliant Integration Strategy and Digital Transformation:
Golden Penny brand has stayed in the heart of many Nigerians for many years. in
2019, the firm won the Standard Organization of Nigeria (SON) high-performance
award and certification. The firm constantly “produces healthy, nutritious, and great-
tasting food for the families” and has expanded its geographical footprints across
many states in Nigeria. This allows it to expand its customer base, accelerate its solid
market share, and ensure the utmost channel of penetration. To meet up with the
industry’s growth level, FMN Plc developed an operating model that prioritizes
innovation and digital transformation. FMN pride itself on the largest food and agro-
allied business in Nigeria. It has recently, through a horizontal integration strategy
acquired a close competitor, Honey Well Plc to increase its market share to c.42%.
With this positive market competitive positioning, we are optimistic that FMN Plc will
maximize shareholders’ returns.

BUSINESS DESCRIPTION
Flour Mills of Nigeria (FMN) Plc is a highly reputable and leading food processing and
agro-allied business in Nigeria with incorporation traced back to 1960. It started
operation in 1962 after its establishment priding the nation’s first wheat mill plant.
The firm is poised to be a key player in achieving Nigeria’s goal of food self-sufficiency
and it has massively invested in 17 modern classic manufacturing facilities since its
establishment spanning through grain milling, edible oil and sugar refining, agro-
inputs, and animal nutrition and proteins thereby expanding its group’s operation
across 12 states. Over the years, it has continually intensified its production capability
and portfolio diversification competence of strong quality and affordable food
products under its evocative brand of Golden Penny of flour, pasta, semolina, sugar,
starch, oil, spreads, and breakfast cereals. FMN Plc also advanced excellently in
support services by way of logistics and distribution, packaging, and port operations.
The firm has also strategically stepped aboard through backward and forward
integration to support its leading position in the market and maintain a positive
competitive advantage.
Business Model
FMN Plc’s business model operates under four business segments that enhance value
creation and value addition of the firm to all its stakeholders viz Food, Agro-Allied,
Sugar, and Support Services. The firm’s business model has continuously expanded to
enhance its winning strategy and meet the diverse needs of consumers and other
stakeholders under four key pillars – (1) continuous product innovation and
improvement, (2) product availability assurance, (3) creation of good consumer
experience, and (4) strong supply chain support. This strategy impressively delivered a
34.48% growth in revenue with a strong net margin of 3.33% in FY- 2021 when
compared to the 0.76% and 1.98% net margin in FY-2019 and FY-2020 respectively. As
FMN Plc increased its geographical footprint across Nigeria, the firm demonstrated
impressive revenue growth of 8.03% CAGR FY2017-FY2021 as revenue from the food
segment captured 61.99% of the entire revenue on average with a mind-blowing
136.87% revenue growth from support service attributable to post-coivd19 economic
expansion. This continued excellence in operating performance of the Food and
support service segment printed an impressive net margin of 2.05% from FY-2017 to
FY-2021. We forecast revenue to grow at 5.38% CAGR. We expect this to have a
moderate impact on the bottom-line with an average net margin of 2.11%.
Food: The chief importance of FMN Plc Revenue is the food division. It has three sub-
division (Pasta and Noodles, Ball Foods, and Flour) while the Golden Penny brand has
successfully established its value in the heart of many Nigerians. This revenue
segment contributes 66.01% to the group’s total revenue between FY-2017 and FY-
2021. It grew by 33.48% in FY-2021 after maintaining resilience against the 2020
pandemic-related shock printing N478 billion compared to N358 billion in FY-2020.

INDUSTRY OVERVIEW
Global Outlook: Food Processing and Wheat
The global food processing industry was valued at $143.51 billion in 2020 with a
growth projection of 6.6% CAGR to the year 2028. According to Verified Market
Research (VMR) , escalating food demand, rising innovation in food processing
machinery, continued research and development, as well as advancements in
technology, will be the key drivers for the market to settle at $235.67 billion by 2028.
The value of Global Flour market was estimated at $258.97 Billion in the year 2021
with a further expectation of growth at 4.60% CAGR to $339.18 Billion by the year
2027 (source: Expert Market Research). This growth is expected to be driven by the
global rising population and a corresponding upsurge in the demand for food.
Russia-Ukraine Impact on the global food industry
2020 was a difficult year on a global scale as Covid-19 impacted the demand-supply
side of the industry. The industry gradually expanded post-covid-19 in 2021, however,
the Russia-Ukraine war exacerbated the supply chain disruption of food products in
2022. Ukraine, a nation regarded as the Breakfast of Europe has 56% arable land of its
total land area. Weforum reported that the country grows abundant food to feed 400
million people globally. Russia and Ukraine account for a huge chunk of global food
export (c.30%) while some countries are particularly dependent on their food
products. For example, about 70% of Turkey’s and Egypt’s wheat export are sourced
from Russia and Ukraine. Harvesting and processing become hindered due to the war
with a resultant effect on prices of inputs and products. In the US, prices of foods for
cereal and bakery products that greatly rely on wheat have skyrocketed, with the
largest increase seen in February 2022. According to FAO Food Price Index (FFPI),
global food prices for March 2022
increased by 12% m/m and 33.6% y/y. We
highlight that the increase was primarily due to a 17.1% y/y increase in the cereal
prices index in line with the Russia-Ukraine crisis. We are of the view that this upward
global price trend will continue to affect input costs as the war continues. We also
expect to see the possible impact on the local food processing industry.

FMN Plc Market Analysis – Competitive Positioning
Competition: We consider FMN Plc to be favorably positioned as one of the leading
top players in the market. The firm has added about 10% to its market share through
the acquisition of Honey Well thereby enabling it to increase its volume. From a
pricing perspective, we understand that the cost of production is high in the industry
as more than 90% of its raw materials are sourced abroad. High freight costs and
continued depreciation of Naira have also propelled cost pressures. We expect the
firm to pass through part of its cost to consumers as one of the market price setters.
We also expect FMN Plc’s strong brand quality to continue to enhance the company’s
business under the “Golden Penny” brand.
Capacity: The industry is largely driven by capacity which is a major determinant of
boosting sales and profitability. Though the industry net margin is slim due to high
direct costs and overhead expenses. FMN Plc reported that they have taken initiatives
to reduce cost, and this has boosted Gross margin and Net margin from 11.47% and
1.98% in 2020 to 13.84% and 3.3%, respectively in 2021. Even though BUA Flour mill
has a capacity of 1,600 metric tonnes per day, they started the construction of 2,600
metric tonnes per day as an addition to its capacity. We do not expect this to have a
significant impact on the performance of FMN Plc. We expect FMN Plc to be more
profitable by out-producing competitors while ensuring a relatively stable cost.

FINANCIAL ANALYSIS
Robust revenue growth in an untapped market:
After an impressive growth of 8.79% y/y for FY-2020, FMN Plc reported stunning
revenue growth of 34.48% y/y for FY-2021 signaling the firm expanding operational
strength to boost its top line. Growth was majorly driven by Food and Agro-allied
segments (33.19% y/y to N617 billion) which comprise 83.16% of the firm’s total
revenue over the past five years (FY2017-FY2021). This performance was enhanced by
a substantial investment of FMN Plc in lucrative local content and route to market
activities thereby maximizing derivable values in the firm’s B2B and B2C businesses.
Our analysis showed that the firm grew its revenue at a CAGR of 8.03% over the five-
year historical period. The firm’s unrelenting innovation and advancement in
marketing strategies with the 2020-2021 Golden Penny Basket campaign have further
supported growth in turnover. Continuing on the ladder of prosperity, FMN Plc
reported N825 billion in 9 months of FY-2022 revenue. This is larger than the overall
revenue reported for FY-2021 by 7.00%. This mind-blowing performance as always
was driven by the food segment with a growth of 55% y/y as core B2C businesses
trend correspond; Flour grew by 24% y/y, Noodles: by 8% y/y, Ball food: by 7% y/y,
and Pasta: by 3%. y/y. Accelerated volumes also impacted this impressive 9-month
growth to meet the ever-increasing demand of consumers. Golden Vita added
2,940MT in volume, Amazing Day – 713MT, Goat Meat pepper soup - 1,000MT in Q3
of the firm’s 2022 fiscal Year-end. The firm also deepened its focus on the small pack
segment of the noodles category to ensure increased affordability of its product.
Drawing from the firm’s commitment to boost local content and its tightened
partnership with the Flour Mills Association of Nigeria to ensure increased agricultural
products and wheat self-sufficiency in Nigeria, we forecast revenue to grow at an
adjusted 5.39% CAGR printing more than N1 trillion by FY-2026E.
Imported price fuel Operating Cost:
Flour Mills Plc operates in an industry that pays an average 80kobo for every N1 of
revenue generated. Hence, the heavy cost to revenue has greatly impacted Gross
margin to record a slim average of 12.16% over the past five years for the firm. Total
cost to sales increased by 30.88% y/y in FY-2021. As expected, a larger part of the
company’s cost of sales goes into the cost of raw and packaging materials at 85.73%
and 87.78% of total cost of sales in FY-2020 and FY-2021, respectively. Given that
more than 90% of the wheat (a primary and large part of raw material) consumed in
Nigeria is imported, we attribute this increase to the after-effect of the pandemic-
related shock that kept the price of wheat and other imported raw materials elevated.
US Wheat Futures as of March 2020 was $ 567.75 per bushel, as the global economy
expanded after the pandemic restrictive measures, the global wheat price jumped by
29.51% in 13 months to $735.3 per bushel by April 2021.

Valuation Summary
We initiate coverage with a BUY recommendation on FMN Plc on a standalone basis.
Our valuation is based on a weighted mix of both Intrinsic and relative valuation
methodologies. We employed Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) and Dividend Discount
Model (DDM) for the intrinsic valuation. For the relative valuation, we used the
valuation methodologies suitable for a firm like FMN Plc as provided by Refinitv viz –
EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, and P/E Ratio. Based on a weight of 70% for the Intrinsic
Valuation and 30% for Comparables (Comps), we derived a 12-month target price of
N51.72 per share. This is a 66.56% upside to the current share price of N31.05 as of
April 01, 2022. The fair value arrived at shows the implied value of FMN Plc. Intrinsic
methods were computed to determine the inherent value of the company from the
perspective of its potential present value of all generatable cash flow. Comps trading
multiples were employed to validate and support our intrinsic methods by looking at
similar public companies with close business and economic fundamentals. Holding to
the idea that the closest comparable for FMN Plc, Olam Flour Mills which acquired
Dangote Flour Mills is unlisted and that scale, key business, and economic attributes
from comparables sourced from Africa and similar companies in Asia to some extent
differs, we decided low weight for our comps multiples.
Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF): For the derivation of FCFF, we used NOPAT allowing us
to project an N38 billion average FCFFF through the forecast period. We are
conservative in our approach by considering possible economic downturn while
expecting aggressive investment in CAPEX. We think that the firm has inherent growth
potential, hence we considered a moderate and appropriate sustainable growth rate
of 11.52% that drives the value of our terminal FCFF. This is equivalent to the than
long-term nominal GDP growth and higher than the long-term inflation rate since we
do not expect the firm to expand beyond the growth of the overall economy while
delivering a return above the rate of inflation.
Capex: Capex is projected to grow at 3.03% on average through the forecast period.

Since the computation of WACC is the weighted result of Cost of Equity and Cost of
Debt, we employed the capital asset pricing model (CAPM - Cost of Equity (Re)= Risk-
free rate+ (beta*Equity risk premium)) for the computation of cost of equity resulting
in a 25.13% (Forecast Horizon) and 17.26% (Terminal Horizon) based on mature
market perspective derivation. Using a US risk-free rate of 2.15% (US Treasury) as well
as adding inflation differential of Nigeria and US plus rating based on default spread
sourced from Damodaran, we arrived at a Naira Risk-Free Rate of 14.06%. The equity
risk premium is an addition to the market risk premium of the mature market and
country risk premium provided by Damodaran. We, however, added alpha to account
for the possible risk that may not be accounted for by these variables. We derived a
risk premium of 9.68%. We used a levered beta of 0.83 estimated from the weighted
average of four calculation methods. For the terminal horizon, we believe that the
real growth rate cannot be lower than the real interest rate since promised returns
must be covered by enough growth in the economy in the long term. Hence, we
applied a cost of equity that is tantamount to the long-term nominal GDP growth rate
of the economy adding a premium based on the risk appetite of investors in a mature
market. We are also of the view that the volatility of the company’s stock against the
market will converge in the long run. Hence, we adopt a beta of one (1).
For the cost of debt, FMN Plc in our view has multiple tranches of debt that
incorporated the total debt size in its capital structure. Hence, picking a yield based on
some of its debts that are listed with different rates of return will not provide a true
cost or required rate of return demanded by debt investors in an arm’s length
transaction. To arrive at a fair value of its debt which consists of both listed and
unlisted debt with different rates of return and maturity, we apply the yield of federal
government bonds with similar FMN longest term to maturity debt of 12.41%. To
account for credit risk, we employed a commercial bond premium calculated as the
average risk premium of similar corporate bonds issued by firms like FMN based on
the data sourced from FMDQ. We consider a default spread of 5.47% from data
sourced from Damodaran with a similar credit rating. FMN credit rating ranges from
CCC-rated Bond which is a lower rating bond with a high inherent risk to B- negative
which is also considered a non-investment grade bond and speculative. We applied
the average spread of this rating as our default spread. To account for liquidity spread,
we used On-The-Run and Off-The-Run FGN Bond spread of 4.94%. We derived an
after-tax cost of debt with an effective tax rate of 32% on average of what the firm
has reported over the historical period.

INVESTMENT RISK
Russia-Ukraine War: The Russia-Ukraine war has triggered the global price of wheat (a
key raw material for flour milling) to rise above $1000 per bushel. This will have a
direct impact on input cost, thus -reducing gross margin. We see this as a significant
risk factor as local prices of wheat-based products have shown a notable uptick over
the past few years and have risen significantly post-covid 19 pandemics.
Mitigation: We understand that Nigeria imports more than 90% of its wheat
consumption and the Russian-Ukraine crisis is an unavoidable risk for all players in the
industry. Even as FMN Plc partners with the Flour Mills Association of Nigeria to
improve wheat production capacity, meeting the level of current consumption will be
a long-term talk. Countries of the world are, however, seeking alternatives to mitigate
the impact of the war going forward through local production expansion and buffer
stock resorts if available. Efforts are ongoing to boost Nigeria’s capacity. We believe
the firm will mitigate this risk through boosting local content, continuous focus on
field monitoring and training of farmers, and buying raw materials 3 months in
advance.
Inflation Risk: Inflationary pressure continues its upward trend to 15.92% in March
2022 maintaining its double digits. Flour Millers products are price sensitive, and
customers tend to seek substitutes when they perceive a possible price hike.
Mitigation: Though prices are set by the Flour Millers Association of Nigeria, FMN Plc
which happens to be one of the market leaders with a significant 42% share can pass
through price to some extent to its customers or through product dynamism strategy.
The firm has tried to support farmers in a strategy that will enable it to localize
contracts. We also think the firm will mitigate this risk by enhancing economies of
scale to increase operational efficiency that reduces cost.
Foreign Exchange Risk: Dollar scarcity has weakened the purchasing power of the
Naira with perceived devaluation in both the official and parallel market trading at
N416 and N580, respectively. A substantial amount of raw materials is sourced abroad
and has to be paid in dollars. Flour Millers now have to go through the black market to
source for dollar to meet their foreign exchange needs at an expensive rate.
Mitigation: We believe the firm is mitigating this risk through backward integration of
its Agro-allied business and exporting of products to neighboring countries – which it
started in 2020. We also expect the firm to leverage the CBN’s intervention in
supporting the industry as well as efforts to ensure Naira’s stability.

13. Fair Value Measurement (IFRS 13)
Russia-Ukraine War
RUSSIAN-UKRAINE WAR IMPACT ON VALUATION
The war in Ukraine has significantly affected individuals, economic
activity, and financial markets on a global scale. The war and the
sanctions imposed have led to a steep jump in commodity prices
(mainly energy, metals, and agricultural commodities) and supply
chain disruptions. Consequently, it is not only companies with
operations (or assets) in Russia, Belarus, or Ukraine, or those
trading with counterparties in these countries, that have been
significantly affected. Other companies may be indirectly affected by
commodity price inflation, shortages in supplies, and a weaker
economic environment – e.g. the automotive, food manufacturing,
agriculture, and transportation sectors.
Fair value is a market-based measurement – it is measured using
assumptions that market participants would use. Determining fair
value in these circumstances has become more challenging due to
the higher uncertainty and elevated risks.
For reporting periods ending after 24 February 2022 (the date
Russia invaded Ukraine), the war is a current-period event that
should be reflected in determining the fair value of assets (and
liabilities) such as debt and equity securities and investment
property, or when testing cash-generating units for impairment
using fair value less costs of disposal. Because fair value is a market-
based measurement (i.e. it is measured using assumptions that
market participants would use), this will become more challenging
due to greater risks and uncertainty.
The recent events may have had a significant impact on the following
risks.
•Country risk: A premium that takes into account the additional
risk associated with generating and incurring cash flows in a
particular country (e.g. Russia, Ukraine).
•Forecasting risk: Fair value measurements should reflect the
greater uncertainty in making economic and financial forecasts in
the near term, due to the difficulty in forecasting the magnitude of
the impact of the war and sanctions imposed.
•
•Illiquidity risk: An additional premium that takes into account
the difficulty of being able to sell an investment.
